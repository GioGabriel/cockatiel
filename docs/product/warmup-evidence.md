# Pre-drill warm-up evidence and product boundary

Cockatiel's pre-drill flow is a short, user-controlled **warm-up and starting-
note explorer**. It is not a vocal-range test, a formal voice classification,
medical therapy, or a scored attempt.

## Decisions

| Decision | Basis | Evidence strength and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offer gentle humming and optional lip-trill-style preparation | National Association of Teachers of Singing (NATS), [The Five Best Vocal Warm-Up Exercises](https://www.nats.org/cgi/page.cgi/5/_articles.html/Pedagogy/The_Five_Best_Vocal_Warm-Up_Exercises) | Established singing pedagogy guidance; not a randomized validation of Cockatiel's exact flow. |
| Let users explore one target at a time with a comfortable volume | NATS guidance and ASHA's [Voice Disorders practice portal](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/voice-disorders/) | Good pedagogical and safety fit; the phone measures acoustic pitch, not airflow, tension, resonance, or vocal health. |
| Use call-and-response, rests, and a three-note starting pattern before the five-note foundation | Product progression decision consistent with isolated target practice and reduced cognitive/load demands | Usability heuristic for beginners; the exact note count and rest length still require user testing and calibration. |
| Make the explorer open-ended but provide an explicit stop action and safety reminder | User-control and safety decision | No evidence supports an unlimited duration as universally beneficial. It must never encourage pushing or shouting. |
| Do not require a warm-up pass before a drill | Prevents a missing or uncomfortable target from becoming a false failure | Product accessibility decision; warm-up observations are guidance only and are excluded from drill scoring. |
| Keep the selected key/octave as the source of truth for every tab | Equal-temperament movable-do resolver shared with target rendering and scoring | Deterministic engineering contract; it does not establish a permanent vocal range. |

## What the research does not establish

Warm-up studies report mixed results and do not identify one routine that is
best for every singer. A comparison of traditional and semi-occluded vocal
tract exercises found no significant difference on the measured outcomes in its
small sample ([Bottalico et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25770376/)). A
later comparison also found no clear superiority between physiological and
traditional warm-ups, while some changes were consistent with early fatigue
([comparison study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28579159/)). A recent adult
choir trial found some favorable outcomes for a four-week tube-phonation
program, but that is not evidence for a universal immediate warm-up or for
Cockatiel's exact dose ([trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41896048/)).

Accordingly, the app uses the warm-up to help the user find a comfortable
starting target and understand the tuner. The 45-second safety reminder,
three-note progression, and lack of a required completion score are product
heuristics, not clinically calibrated thresholds.

## Safety and measurement boundary

- Use a normal, comfortable volume; never ask the user to shout.
- Stop immediately for pain, strain, dizziness, hoarseness, or discomfort.
- Persistent voice symptoms should be handled by a qualified voice professional
  or clinician; the app does not diagnose or treat voice conditions.
- The microphone estimates fundamental frequency and signal quality only. It
  cannot directly measure airflow, breath pressure, resonance, throat tension,
  vocal-fold health, or singing quality.
- Warm-up frames are not sent as a scored attempt and are not uploaded as raw
  audio to the AI provider.
